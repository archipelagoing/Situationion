## Next Steps

### 1. Freeze the Current v3 Results

Keep the current experiments as the baseline for future comparisons.

- Keep the failed mean-pooled situation score.
- Keep the attention-span analysis.
- Keep the original alternative-readout experiment.
- Keep the lexically controlled readout experiment.
- Do not tune these experiments to produce the originally predicted layer 23–26 effect.

---

### 2. Fix the Remaining Structural Confound

Create stricter base–paraphrase–counterfactual triples where surface similarity is approximately matched between conditions.

Control for:

- Unigram overlap
- Bigram overlap
- Sentence length
- Syntactic structure
- Approximate edit distance

The goal is:

\[
\text{SurfaceSim}(B,P) \approx \text{SurfaceSim}(B,C)
\]

while maintaining:

\[
\text{Situation}(B)=\text{Situation}(P)
\]

and

\[
\text{Situation}(B)\neq\text{Situation}(C)
\]

This will make it substantially harder for lexical or structural similarity to explain the situation-separation effect.

---

### 3. Build a Larger Controlled Dataset

Expand from the current small pipeline-check dataset to approximately **200–500 controlled triples**.

Balance examples across situation manipulations such as:

- Agent/recipient changes
- Causal changes
- Temporal changes
- Polarity changes
- Event/state changes

Use multiple:

- Sentence templates
- Entity names
- Objects
- Verbs
- Surface constructions

The dataset should be designed before examining model results to avoid selecting examples that produce a desired layer pattern.

---

### 4. Create Held-Out Evaluation Splits

Separate the dataset by entire lexical/template families rather than randomly splitting individual sentences.

Create:

- Development templates
- Held-out test templates
- Development entity sets
- Held-out entity sets

This will test whether observed situation representations generalize beyond specific wording patterns.

---

### 5. Rerun All Representation Readouts

On the larger controlled dataset, calculate the situation-separation score across all 48 GPT-2 XL layers:

\[
S(l)=
\cos(h_l(B),h_l(P))
-
\cos(h_l(B),h_l(C))
\]

Compare:

- Mean pooling
- Max pooling
- Final-token representation
- Changed-token representation
- Event-verb representation
- Agent/recipient representation

The primary question is no longer simply whether a bump occurs at layers 23–26.

Instead ask:

> Where does reliable situation separation emerge, where is it strongest, and how long does it persist?

---

### 6. Add Statistical Uncertainty

Add statistical analysis to the layer-wise curves.

Calculate:

- Bootstrap confidence intervals
- Standard errors
- Effect sizes
- Reliability of separation above zero

Determine:

- First reliably positive layer
- Strongest layer or region
- Duration of the effect
- Whether layers 23–26 are significantly stronger than neighboring layers

Do not infer a special region from visual inspection alone.

---

### 7. Analyze Situation Dimensions Separately

Instead of averaging all counterfactual manipulations together, calculate separate layer-wise curves for:

- Agent/recipient
- Causality
- Temporal relations
- Polarity
- Event/state changes

Ask whether different components of a situation become represented at different depths.

A possible outcome is that there is no single universal "situation layer," but rather different representational trajectories for different situation dimensions.

---

### 8. Investigate the Layer 8–12 Transition

The current controlled experiment suggests that strong final-token situation separation begins emerging around the early-to-middle transition.

Treat this as a **new hypothesis generated by v3**, not as a replacement for the original prediction.

Original hypothesis:

> **H1:** Situation representations should show a privileged regime around layers 23–26.

New exploratory hypothesis:

> **H2:** Situation-sensitive contextual information begins emerging near the early-to-middle transition and persists across a broader middle-layer region.

Test H2 independently on the expanded held-out dataset.

---

### 9. Compare Situation Emergence With Attention Span

Compare the emergence of situation separation with the previously observed transition toward broader attention.

Ask:

> Does increasing situation sensitivity systematically coincide with increasing contextual attention span?

Treat this relationship as correlational.

Attention patterns can provide supporting evidence about contextual integration but cannot establish that attention causes situation representations.

---

### 10. Run Layer-Wise Probes

Once the larger controlled dataset is established, train probes for specific situation variables.

Probe for:

- Agent identity
- Recipient identity
- Event information
- Polarity
- Causal relations
- Temporal relations

Evaluate probes using held-out lexical templates and entities.

This tests:

> Where in the network is each component of the situation reliably decodable?

---

### 11. Add Projection Controls

Compare PCA visualizations against random projections.

Use:

- PCA
- Random projection
- Multiple random seeds

This tests whether apparent geometric structure is meaningful or simply a consequence of projecting high-dimensional residual-stream representations into two dimensions.

---

### 12. Add Stronger Non-Semantic Controls

Introduce additional controls designed to separate semantic effects from surface-form effects.

Possible controls include:

- Lexically matched nonsensical sentences
- Same syntax with shuffled semantic roles
- Same vocabulary with altered relational meaning
- Token-position controls
- Structurally matched meaning-changing sentences

These controls should progressively eliminate alternative explanations for the observed geometry.

---

### 13. Select Candidate Layers

Only after the large-dataset geometry and probing experiments are complete, identify regions for causal testing.

Select:

- Early-layer control region
- Empirically strongest situation-sensitive region
- Late-layer control region

Do not automatically select layer 24 because it was part of the original hypothesis.

---

### 14. Run Activation Patching

Use activation patching to determine whether the identified representations are actually used by the model.

Compare patches from:

- Situation-preserving donors
- Situation-changing donors
- Early-layer controls
- Candidate situation-sensitive layers
- Late-layer controls
- Random/noise controls
- Lexical controls

Measure whether patching changes relevant next-token probabilities in the predicted direction.

This changes the research question from:

> "Is situation information readable here?"

to:

> "Does GPT-2 actually use the situation information represented here?"

---

### 15. Test Causal Specificity

If activation patching produces an effect, determine whether that effect is specific to the manipulated situation variable.

For example:

- Patching agent information should preferentially alter agent-consistent predictions.
- Patching recipient information should preferentially alter recipient-consistent predictions.
- Patching temporal information should preferentially alter temporal predictions.
- Patching polarity information should preferentially alter predictions affected by whether an event occurred.

A general disruption of model output is not sufficient evidence for a situation-specific causal mechanism.

---

### 16. Replicate the Causal Results

Repeat successful causal experiments across:

- Different examples
- Different templates
- Different entity sets
- Different manipulation categories
- Relevant experimental seeds

The causal effect should generalize rather than depend on a small number of specially selected prompts.

---

### 17. Make the Final Decision About the SituatiONION Hypothesis

After completing the geometry, controls, probes, attention analysis, and causal interventions, evaluate the original hypothesis.

Possible conclusions include:

**A. Localized Onion**

Layers 23–26 form a uniquely important situation-processing regime.

**B. Broad Onion**

Situation-sensitive representations emerge earlier and persist across a broad middle-layer region rather than forming a narrow layer band.

**C. Distributed Onion**

Different situation variables emerge or peak at different depths, with no single universal situation-processing region.

**D. Readable but Non-Causal**

Situation information can be decoded from particular representations, but interventions fail to demonstrate that the model causally relies on those representations.

**E. Surface/Architectural Effect**

After sufficiently strict controls, the apparent situation-sensitive geometry substantially disappears, suggesting that earlier results were primarily driven by lexical, structural, positional, or architectural factors.

All five outcomes are scientifically meaningful results.
